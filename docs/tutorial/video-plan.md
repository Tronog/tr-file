# tr-file — video tutorial: scene plan

PRD 016, Section 3.1. A second PowerPoint, one feature per slide, to become a short YouTube video with no voice-over: each slide is a picture of the app with subtitle-style captions, at a fast reading pace. Remote servers and the desktop app are left out — but for S02's `Ctrl`+`` ` `` (Section 3.1.1), which the caption says is the desktop app's.

**40 scenes · 5:19** · 22 pictures start from the Section 1 screenshots, 18 are new (1 of them drawn on the slide).

## The rules every slide follows

- **One feature per slide.** Its name small at the top left, the picture filling the slide, the feature's keys as keycaps in a corner.
- **One highlight.** A moment after the slide appears the rest of the window dims, leaving the part the captions are about.
- **Captions like subtitles.** Bottom centre, white on a dark band, at most 2 lines of 42 characters, a short sentence or phrase each, one after another.
- **A fast reading pace.** A caption stays up for its length at 17 characters a second (the fast end of what subtitle guidelines allow), and never less than 1.8 s. Each slide adds 2.4 s before its first caption — to take the picture in while the highlight fades in — and 1.2 s after its last.
- **1920 × 1080.** The window is captured at 1600 × 900 and scaled 1.2×, so text stays sharp at 1080p.
- **Silent, unbranded.** No voice, no music, no intro or logo: the video opens straight on S01 and ends on the last caption.
- **What a browser shows.** The pictures are of tr-file in a browser, so no caption names a key the browser keeps for itself (`Ctrl`+`T`, `Ctrl`+`W`, `Ctrl`+`Tab`, `Ctrl`+`PageUp`/`PageDown`, `Ctrl`+`1`…`9`).

## Making it

1. **Pictures.** The Section 1 capture script at 1600 × 900 and 1.2×, with one spotlight instead of numbered labels; each scene saved twice, plain and highlighted.
2. **The deck.** The Section 2 generator: one slide per scene, the two pictures stacked so the highlight fades in, the captions as text boxes appearing in turn, and each slide's timing set from this plan.
3. **The video.** PowerPoint's *Export › Create a Video* at 1080p, using the slide timings. Without PowerPoint, the same timings drive ffmpeg over the slide pictures, with the captions burned in.
4. **For YouTube:** the captions as an `.srt` file too, so viewers can turn them off or have them translated; the chapters in the description; a thumbnail from S01.

## Built

In `video/`: `tr-file-video.pptx` (one slide per scene, timed: pictures fade in, captions come and go, each slide advances by itself), `tr-file-video.mp4` (1920 × 1080, 30 fps, silent, rendered from the same layout), `tr-file-video.srt` (the captions, for YouTube), and `images/` (each scene's frames, plain then highlighted; `frames.json` lists them).
